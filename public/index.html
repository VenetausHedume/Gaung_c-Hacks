The problem

Pharmacy retail in India runs mostly on trust — and that trust gets broken quietly.

None of this gets reported, because there's never been anywhere to report it to.

01

Sold above MRP

Retailers quietly mark up essential medicines, counting on buyers not to check the printed price.

02

Stock that should exist, doesn't

Scheduled and essential medicines go missing from shelves with no record of why, or for how long.

03

Expired stock still sold

Without public tracking, expired batches stay on shelves until a customer happens to notice.

How it works

Three steps, and the record is public.

STEP 01

Scan and submit

Scan the medicine's barcode, then enter or photograph the price you were billed. MediCheck checks it against the real MRP on record.

STEP 02

A mismatch becomes a report

If the price, stock or expiry doesn't check out, a report is filed with your bill photo attached — visible to anyone who looks up that pharmacy.

STEP 03

Government and pharmacy respond, in public

The local authority posts progress on the case. The pharmacy can clear its name by keeping its billing and stock records open to verification.

Questions

Before you file a report

Do I need the physical medicine strip to scan?+

Yes — the barcode on the strip or box is what's checked against the registered MRP. A photo of the barcode works if you've already left the counter.

What happens after I submit a report?+

It's published immediately with your bill photo, and routed to the health authority for that pharmacy's area. You can track progress from your dashboard.

Can a pharmacy see who filed a report?+

No. Pharmacies see the report, the bill and the mismatch — never your name or contact details.

How do pharmacies become verified?+

By regularly uploading their stock and billing records. Once those records match what citizens are reporting on the ground, the pharmacy is marked verified.

Found a price that doesn't add up?

It takes less time to report than it took to notice.
